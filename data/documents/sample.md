# The Axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum)

The axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) is an amphibian endemic to the lake system of the Valley of Mexico. It belongs to the family of ambystomatids (salamanders) and has an extraordinary trait: it retains its larval characteristics even in adulthood, a condition known as neoteny. This means it keeps its tadpole-like dorsal fin that runs almost the entire length of its body and its feathery external gills protruding from the back of its wide head, rather than undergoing the usual metamorphosis that transforms other amphibians for terrestrial life.

## Amazing Regeneration

The most fascinating biological aspect of the axolotl is its regenerative capacity. While other species can regenerate limbs or tails, the axolotl is capable of regenerating entire amputated limbs, and if injured, it can also regenerate parts of its heart, spinal cord, and even portions of its brain. This capability leaves no scars and allows the new limb or organ to function exactly like the original. For this reason, the axolotl is one of the most studied animal models in laboratories worldwide, with the hope of understanding these cellular mechanisms and eventually applying them in human regenerative medicine.

## Habitat and Conservation Status

Historically, axolotls populated the lakes of Texcoco and Xochimilco. Today, their natural habitat is almost exclusively reduced to the canals of Xochimilco in southern Mexico City.

Unfortunately, the axolotl is critically endangered in the wild, according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN). The main threats include:
1. **Habitat loss:** Uncontrolled urbanization and the draining of lakes have drastically reduced its natural environment.
2. **Pollution:** Poor water quality due to wastewater discharges affects its survival.
3. **Invasive species:** The introduction of exotic fish such as tilapia and carp, which compete for food and prey on axolotl eggs.

It is ironic that while the species is going extinct in its natural habitat, there are millions of specimens bred in captivity in laboratories and aquariums around the world due to their scientific importance.
